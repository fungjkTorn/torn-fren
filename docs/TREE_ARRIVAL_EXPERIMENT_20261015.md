# Fixed nonlinear arrival-stock experiment

The previous inventory-feature experiment found that adding quantity and observed selling speed to a fixed neighbor distance did not broadly improve useful departure outcomes. This experiment learns nonlinear feature combinations with a small deterministic tree ensemble. It is a new research family, not a production change.

Predict sampled stock ≥30 at a standard-flight-plus-wait horizon. Do not predict future batch quantities, chain median cycles, or inherit restock/depletion event accuracy. Physical depletion remains positive→exactly zero; stock1–29 stays physically positive. Current-rate safeguards and local inventory features are unchanged from the previous ablation.

Inputs: the14 inventory/cycle/time features, absolute lead, lead relative to recent completed-cycle duration, first two sine/cosine harmonics of projected phase, and arrival time-of-day sine/cosine (22 total). Each hourly origin supplies up to33 lead labels on a15-minute grid through eight hours of waiting. A label is eligible only after its arrival plus190-second evidence allowance. Unknown/pending outcomes do not become negative labels. Each origin's total training weight is at most one even when many horizons are known; origins and stock episodes remain correlated.

Fit one fixed ensemble of24 depth-two logistic Newton trees, learning rate0.15, leaf regularization0.5, at most eight quantile bins per feature, leaf values clipped±2, at least24 rows and3 decayed origin-weight units per child. Bins are fitted only to eligible training rows. No per-item hyperparameter search, random seed selection, package installation or model selection on the final evaluation period.

Two predeclared schedules refit at canonical UTC12-hour boundaries: stable uses the last14 days with seven-day weight half-life; recent uses seven days with two-day half-life. Both require at least48 distinct hourly origins and20 labels from each class. Training uses retained pre-window history, and current queries do not warm up a hidden online learner. Individual short-horizon labels can enter once mature without requiring their origin's entire eight-hour trajectory to finish.

Each schedule has two fixed departure rules: earliest120-second bin with smoothed score≥0.70, or earliest within0.02 of the supported peak with0.15 floor. Three-adjacent-bin smoothing applies. Model scores are explicitly uncalibrated. No score is a certified success probability. The120-second query grid matches the recent inventory-path experiment; the earlier logistic baseline used300 seconds, so this comparison does not isolate architecture alone. It also differs from whole-path analogs by using individually mature labels. Report those distinctions rather than attributing every difference to trees.

Four new policies run on the same eight weak items plus Jaguar and Peony controls. Compare against all established references, the earlier direct logistic classifier, and the phase/quantity/flow ablations on the same snapshot. Primary travel metrics remain all five-minute starts pooled, coverage, waiting time, UNKNOWN/PENDING/ABSTAIN counts, matched outcomes, UTC hourly,12 UTC-minute offsets and chronological consistency. Keep every fixed variant, including failures and zero-departure cases.

Tests cover deterministic nonlinear learning, whole-future and later-label feature poisoning, shorter snapshot/prefix invariance, canonical refit independence from earlier queries, mature labels, stale polls, unknowns and stock20 remaining physically positive. Full replay parity/read-only checks also apply. Any useful historical result requires separate future validation. Neither the original frozen window nor the separately fixed Chamois source/window is modified.
