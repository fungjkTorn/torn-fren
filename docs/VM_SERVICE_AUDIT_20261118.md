**Checkpoint.** The read-only inventory and dependency trace are complete, with a second live check at 7:21 p.m. EST. Keep the production publishers and forward exporter. ## Scope and evidence

The audit used verified SSH, systemd unit and timer metadata, filesystem and process summaries, aggregate journal counts, SQLite queries opened read-only, deployed source inspection, and HTTPS GETs through the VM's Nginx listener with TLS verification. Service state, file ages, and counts are point-in-time observations. The deployed `/experimental` wiring was newer than the local VM-source checkout, so the deployed code and live responses govern this report. See the earlier [November 12 dependency checkpoint](VM_SERVICE_DEPENDENCIES_20261112.md) for historical context.

- Nginx, Torn-FREN web, poller, Discord bot, and the separate arbitrage service were running. Nginx sends the primary website to the web process and arbitrage requests to the separate arbitrage process. Scheduled backups were enabled and five backup files were present.
- At 7:21 p.m. EST the VM had 2 vCPUs, 11 GiB RAM with 9.3 GiB available, no swap, and 29 GiB free on the 45 GiB root disk. There were 12 enabled and five disabled Torn-FREN timers.
- The live beta tree used about 3.4 GiB. Its ten versioned release directories together used about 93 MiB. Active and historical forecast ledgers account for much of the remaining space. Disk usage does not warrant an immediate retirement.
- At 7:21 p.m. EST, `/`, `/experimental`, `/travel-decisions`, `/api/first-release`, and `/api/beanstalk-hot-list` all returned HTTP 200. The Hot List returned seven picks. The forecast board returned 24 rows across four cohorts, with 10 incumbents, six challengers, and six next-six rows in fresh cohorts and two final-pair rows marked `STALE_DATA`. An earlier check also verified the same cohort structure at `/api/travel-decisions` and `/api/experimental/v41/predictions`.
- Six consecutive successful collector heartbeats at the recheck were 29–30 seconds apart. The queue query found one completed and three pending recovery jobs, with up to 3,406 attempts, and six completed and 479 pending routine jobs.

## Proposed sequence and rollback

1. **Capture a baseline.** Record each candidate timer's enabled and active state, effective unit and drop-ins, live publisher revisions, and output and queue ages. Confirm owners for private consumers. Before any archival, preserve a consistent backup of affected SQLite files and a manifest of paths, ownership, and permissions.
2. **Trial-pause the V48 timer only.** In a separately authorized change, stop the timer and let an in-flight oneshot finish. Leave cache and unit artifacts in place. Observe several publisher cycles. Check collector heartbeats, per-cohort status and departure freshness, all seven Hot List picks, graph history, and Discord stock and V2 commands. Compare final-pair against its already-stale baseline. **Rollback:** start the timer again and restore its recorded enablement if it was changed. Confirm the timer fires and rerun the checks.
3. **Authorize private research changes individually.** If the five-minute experiment is complete, pause its timer before considering the 30-minute exporter. Verify off-VM workflow and manual consumers before pausing the exporter. **Rollback:** restore the 30-minute exporter first if its fallback is needed, wait for a fresh output, then restore the five-minute timer and validate issue and report freshness. Preserve prospective evidence throughout.
4. **Handle production repairs separately.** Reproduce the final-pair grading exception and recovery queue timeout against a protected copy with bounded runs before changing the VM. For recovery, verify jobs complete, the routine backlog falls, and collector cadence holds. For final-pair, verify both rows stay fresh through consecutive runs and the history summaries still work. **Rollback:** restore the recorded publisher or worker revision and unit configuration if a repair regresses. Preserve the current database and evidence rather than replacing it with an older snapshot.
5. **Archive only releases with verified references.** Check effective unit and wrapper references, private tooling, and documented rollback targets. Use a reversible archive with a manifest, starting with superseded challenger and staging directories. **Rollback:** restore each archived artifact to its original path and ownership, then verify any affected consumer.

## Limits
